# Astronaut Missions Analysis (1961 to 2026)

## Question

How has the number of countries sending people to space changed since 1961?

**Sub-questions:**

1. How do countries without their own human launch rockets get their astronauts to space?
2. How has participation in human spaceflight by countries outside the three launch nations (the US, the USSR/Russia, and China) changed since 1961?

## Data

My data comes from two sources:

- **1961 to early 2020:** a public astronaut database published by Mariya Stavnichuk and Tatsuya Corlett in 2020. I got it from [TidyTuesday](https://github.com/rfordatascience/tidytuesday), a public GitHub repo that publishes clean datasets for people to practice on.
- **Early 2020 to 2026:** this dataset stops in early 2020, and answering my question requires the most current data. For missions flown after that, I'm collecting information from public Wikipedia pages.

## Scope

I included all orbital flights from 1961 to 2026. 1961 is when the first human orbital flight happened, and going through 2026 gives me the most up-to-date picture.

I chose not to include suborbital flights. Most recent suborbital flights are commercial trips that aren't tied to a national space agency or research program, so they reflect space tourism more than national participation in spaceflight.

## Why this matters

Space is one of the last true frontiers. Making human spaceflight accessible to more countries means more people, ideas, and global resources directed toward space research.
